## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes an electronics store sales dataset using Microsoft Excel to understand sales trends, product performance, regional strength, customer contribution, and pricing behavior. The analysis combines pivot tables, charts, dashboards, and correlation analysis to support data-driven conclusions.

---

## 🎯 Objectives
- Analyze monthly and quarterly sales trends  
- Identify top revenue-generating product categories  
- Evaluate the relationship between quantity sold, revenue, and price  
- Assess city-level sales performance  
- Identify key revenue-driving orders  
- Determine peak sales periods  

---

## ❓ Research Questions
1. Does sales increase or decrease by month or quarter?  
2. Which product categories generate the highest sales revenue?  
3. Do products with high quantities always lead to high revenue?  
4. Which cities show the strongest sales performance?  
5. Which orders drive the most revenue?  
6. What are the peak sales periods?  
7. Do higher-priced products sell fewer units?  

---

## 🧰 Tools Used
- Microsoft Excel  
- Pivot Tables and Pivot Charts  
- Scatter Plots with Trendlines  
- Correlation Analysis  
- Interactive Dashboards and Slicers  

---

## 🔍 Methodology
- Cleaned and structured the dataset (date–time separation and calculated sales fields)  
- Built pivot tables for time, product, city, and order analysis  
- Visualized patterns using column charts, line charts, and scatter plots  
- Applied correlation analysis to validate visual findings  

---

## 📈 Key Insights (Including Correlation)
- Sales increase toward the end of the year, with **Q4 recording the highest total sales**  
- A small number of product categories account for most of the revenue  
- **Quantity Ordered vs Total Sales:** Correlation = **-0.398**, indicating a weak negative relationship; high quantities do not always lead to high revenue  
- **Price Each vs Quantity Ordered:** Correlation ≈ **-0.148**, showing a very weak negative relationship; higher-priced products tend to sell fewer units, but price alone does not strongly determine demand  
- San Francisco is the strongest-performing city by total sales  
- Revenue is concentrated among a few high-value orders  

---

## 📊 Dashboard Highlights
- Monthly and quarterly sales trend analysis  
- Product category revenue comparison  
- Quantity vs revenue scatter plot with R² = 0.1586  
- Price vs quantity scatter plot with weak negative correlation  
- City-wise sales performance  
- Cumulative sales contribution by Order ID  
- Interactive slicers for filtering  

---

## 📌 Conclusion
The analysis shows that sales performance is driven more by seasonality, pricing, and product value than by sales volume alone. Strong regional demand and a small number of high-value orders contribute significantly to total revenue.

---

## 🚀 Recommendations
- Focus sales campaigns and inventory planning around Q4  
- Prioritize high-revenue product categories over high-volume items  
- Strengthen presence in top-performing cities such as San Francisco  
- Apply balanced pricing strategies to optimize revenue  
- Target high-value customers and orders for sustained growth
